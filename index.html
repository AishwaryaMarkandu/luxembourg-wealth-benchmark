Executive summary

Growth is strong. The operating model behind it is under strain.

Finding 1

Record assets, thin margins

Assets grew by €94bn in 2024, yet private banks still run a 72.7% average cost-to-income ratio against 55.8% for universal banks.

Finding 2

Automation is the widest gap

Archetype scores on automation range from 3.2 to 4.6. That 1.4-point spread is the largest across the eight dimensions.

Finding 3

Efficiency alone does not win

The most efficient archetype (B, 4.4) comes last on the balanced composite (3.82). The leader (C, 4.29) pairs digital, data and automation.

Step 1 · Market

A record year for assets, and pressure on the model that earns from them

Luxembourg is the largest private banking centre in the European Union. Assets jumped in 2024, while costs have grown faster than commissions and the client base has moved up the wealth ladder.

Five forces on the operating model

  • Intergenerational transferHeirs expect transparency, hybrid digital journeys, and access to private markets and sustainable solutions (ABBL, 2026).
  • Cost pressureIn 2023, operating expenses rose 8.5% while commission income fell 1.2% (KPMG-ABBL 2024).
  • Digital competitionGlobal wealth platforms and fintech entrants offer seamless access to alternatives (PwC Luxembourg, via Paperjam).
  • Regulation as a catalystDORA is seen as a lever for transformation, not only a compliance cost (same source).
  • ConsolidationThe number of private banks keeps falling, almost entirely through consolidation (Paperjam interview with the PBGL chair).

Step 3 · Client segments

The client base has moved up the wealth ladder

Assets from clients with under €1m fell from 24% to 5% of the total in thirteen years. Reaching the next generation of wealth now takes a different entry model, not a thinner version of the private banking one.

What it means

  • Concentration at the topAssets are increasingly held by a smaller number of larger relationships, which raises the cost of losing one.
  • A thin entry tierA first private banking experience for emerging wealth is still immature in Luxembourg, according to industry commentary.
  • Next-gen entrepreneursTechnology founders realise wealth earlier, which shifts the profile of the future client.
